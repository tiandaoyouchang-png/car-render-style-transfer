# Commercial and automotive appearance

Describe light transport rather than prestige words. A broad key creates long controlled highlights;
a restrained fill keeps shadow-side features readable without flattening form; flags define clean
dark-to-light transitions. Base this hierarchy on the current environment, not a canned studio setup.

For paint, distinguish body color from reflected environment; use believable clearcoat and controlled
microflake only when consistent with the brief. Glass needs plausible transmission, thickness and
reflections, not opaque black plastic or blown-out white. Rubber is materially different from glossy
trim. Metals retain coherent specular behavior. Avoid drawing bright outlines on every edge.

Preserve vehicle identifiers and design features from the shape reference. Do not copy its old light,
color grade, road or shadow. Do not invent an emblem, license plate slogan or headlamp topology.
Retain requested finishes; do not make every surface reflective just to look premium.

Match ground contact shadows, reflection direction and color temperature to the scene. Ask for a
controlled highlight rolloff and readable shadow details, not contradictory all-over HDR contrast.
No unrequested smoke, wet road, sunset, flare, bloom, advertising typography or decorative props.
Use only enough detail to resolve ambiguity; long prompts do not automatically make better images.
